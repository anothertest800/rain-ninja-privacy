Rain Ninja privacy policy

In force from 24 November 2026. It covers Rain Ninja for Android, version 4.20.0 and later.

Rain Ninja is a motorcycle ride planner with live rain radar. This policy says what information the app uses, where that information goes, and what you can do about it. The same text is included in the app (Settings, at the bottom: Privacy policy) and published at https://anothertest800.github.io/rain-ninja-privacy/.

The short version

  • No account, no adverts, no analytics and no trackers.
  • The developer runs no server for the app and receives none of your information: not your location, not your rides, not your settings.
  • Your rides, your home, your settings and your ride history stay on your phone.
  • To show forecasts, radar, maps and places, the app asks public services on the internet. Those requests carry a rounded position or the text you typed, and, like every internet request, your phone's IP address.
  • Your location is used while you use the app. It is used in the background only if you turn on a Nanny, and only after you agree on a separate screen.

Who is responsible

Rain Ninja is made by an independent developer, who publishes on GitHub as anothertest800 ("we" in this policy). To ask a question or make a request, open an issue at github.com/anothertest800/rain-ninja-privacy/issues. That page is public: please do not write personal details in it.

What the app uses

Your location

The app uses your precise or approximate location, whichever you allow in Android.

  • While you use the app. The radar screen centres the map on you and counts the minutes until rain reaches you. The planner can start a ride where you are. Ride with radar follows you on the map and speaks rain warnings; it keeps doing so with the screen off, and Android shows a notification for as long as it runs.
  • In the background, only with a Nanny on. Rain Ninja collects location data to enable the Nanny's go-home reminders (the dusk Nanny and the Rain nanny) even when the app is closed or not in use. While a Nanny is on, the app reads the phone's position at each check (every 1 to 30 minutes, as you set it), works out the way home on the phone, and asks the weather service for the forecast along that way. Android shows a notification for as long as a Nanny runs. The app asks you first, on a screen of its own, before Android's permission screen. If you say no, everything else works as before. Turning both Nannies off stops it.

Your location is never sent to the developer and is never used for advertising. Where it does go is listed under What leaves your phone.

What you type and save

The places or postcodes you type as a start or a home, the rides you save, and your settings (look, text size, road choices, weather source, Nanny and Route watch settings). All of it is stored on your phone. A place name or postcode you type is sent to a lookup service to find where it is.

Ride history

While "Keep a ride history on this phone" is on (Settings, Rain dodge; it is on when the app is new), the app writes down where you are every 30 seconds while Ride with radar is on, and at each Nanny check. It uses this to tell which way you are going. The history is kept for 14 days in the app's private storage, is not part of any backup and never leaves the phone. You can switch it off or clear it in the same place.

The app's log

The app keeps a small log (about 1 MB at most) in its private storage: what it did, errors and crashes. The log is not sent anywhere. You can read and copy it in the ride screen's settings; it leaves the phone only if you copy it and share it yourself.

What the app does not use

Your name, e-mail address, phone number, contacts, photos, files, microphone or camera; an advertising ID or any other device identifier; payment details. There is nothing to buy in the app.

What leaves your phone

The developer has no server, so nothing goes to the developer. The app asks the services below directly, over an encrypted connection (HTTPS). Each of them sees your phone's IP address and the usual technical details of a web request, and handles them under its own privacy policy. The app sends no account, no advertising ID and no device identifier with these requests.

Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com): weather forecasts and wind
Receives: positions rounded to a grid of about 11 km: your start, points along the routes being checked, the saved rides you watch, and, for a Nanny, your position and your home. For the wind arrow on the radar, your position rounded to about 1 km.
When: planning a ride, Ride with radar, Route watch and Nanny checks (these two also in the background, if you turned them on).
Open-Meteo terms and privacy
MET Norway (api.met.no): weather forecasts
Receives: positions rounded to about 5 km.
When: only when you choose MET Norway as the weather source, or when Open-Meteo does not answer.
MET Norway privacy
OpenStreetMap Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org): place search
Receives: the place name you typed.
When: the app has to find a start or a home that you gave as a place name, not as a UK postcode.
OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy
postcodes.io and getthedata.com: UK postcodes
Receives: the UK postcode you typed.
When: the app has to find a start or a home that you gave as a UK postcode.
postcodes.io (Ideal Postcodes) privacy · getthedata.com privacy
OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org): the map
Receives: the numbers of the map squares on your screen, which show roughly what area you are looking at.
When: a map is on screen and that area is not in an offline map you downloaded.
OpenFreeMap privacy policy
Rain radar stores: Met Office and NOAA on Amazon S3, EUMETNET OPERA at CloudFerro and ECMWF, Iowa State University
Receives: which radar picture is wanted. A picture covers a whole country or continent, so no position is sent.
When: whenever the app shows or uses the rain radar.
Amazon Web Services privacy · CloudFerro privacy · ECMWF privacy · Iowa State University terms
GitHub (github.com): offline maps and routing roads
Receives: which map or road file is downloaded (a country or a region).
When: you download or update offline maps or routing roads. GitHub shows the developer only how many times each file was downloaded.
GitHub privacy statement

Routes are worked out on the phone, with the routing roads you downloaded. No route and no position is sent to a routing service.

The developer-mode weather test (Settings, Developer; off unless you turn it on) sends your start position to the weather service without rounding.

The app also contains a speed-limit and places-of-interest lookup that would ask OpenStreetMap's Overpass servers (overpass-api.de, overpass.private.coffee) and Nominatim about a position. No screen of this version uses it. If a later version does, this policy will say so first.

Other apps and Android features you may choose to use

  • Google Maps. When you tap a Google Maps button on a route, the points of that route are handed to Google Maps, which works under Google's privacy policy.
  • Export and import. When you export rides or a GPX file, the app writes a file where you choose. What happens to that file afterwards is up to you.
  • Voice. Spoken warnings are made by the text-to-speech engine installed on your phone. The app hands it the words to speak.
  • Position on the ride screen. It comes from Google Play services on your phone, under Google's privacy policy.
  • Android backup. If backup is on in Android's settings, Android stores the app's settings (including your Nanny home), your saved rides and the planner's stored rides and settings in your own Google account, and can copy them to a new phone. The ride history, the log, offline maps and routing roads are not backed up. You manage this backup in Android's and Google's settings.

The permissions the app asks for

  • Location (precise or approximate): the radar around you, a start where you are, Ride with radar.
  • Location all the time (background): the Nanny only. See above.
  • Notifications: the running ride, the running Nanny and its go-home alerts, Route watch.
  • Foreground service (location): keeps a ride or a Nanny running with the screen off, always with a notification.
  • Alarms and reminders: wakes the phone for each Nanny check on time.
  • Keep awake, vibrate: finishing a check while the phone sleeps, and the alert.
  • Internet, network and Wi-Fi state: the downloads listed above, and telling Wi-Fi from mobile data before a large download.

The app may also offer to open Android's battery settings, so that you can stop Android putting the app to sleep during a ride. That choice is yours and is made in Android's own screen.

How long things are kept, and how to delete them

  • Saved rides, your home and your settings stay on the phone until you delete them: delete a ride in the app, or turn a Nanny off.
  • The ride history is kept for 14 days. Settings, Rain dodge has "Clear the ride history" and the switch to stop keeping one.
  • Downloaded forecasts, radar pictures and map squares are kept for a short time and replaced automatically. Offline maps and routing roads stay until you delete them in Settings.
  • To delete everything at once: Android Settings, Apps, Rain Ninja, Storage, Clear storage; or uninstall the app.
  • A backup in your Google account, and files you exported, are yours to delete where they are.

The developer holds no information about you, so there is nothing for the developer to send you, correct or delete.

Security

Every connection to an outside service is encrypted (HTTPS). The app shows its planner through a small web server inside the app; it listens on the phone itself only, and answers only this app, with a secret key that is new at every start. The app's files are in Android's private storage for the app.

Children

Rain Ninja is made for motorcycle riders. It is not aimed at children, and the developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the UK or EU General Data Protection Regulation), you may have the right to see, correct, move or delete information about you, and to object to its use. Because your information stays on your phone, you can do all of that yourself, in the app and in Android's settings. For what a service listed above holds (for example its record of a request from your IP address), please ask that service; its privacy policy is linked above. You can also complain to your data-protection authority; in the United Kingdom that is the Information Commissioner's Office.

Other countries

The services listed above run in several countries, among them the United Kingdom, the European Union, Norway and the United States. A request from your phone may therefore be handled outside your own country.

Changes to this policy

When the app changes what it uses or where it sends it, this page and the copy in the app change with it, and the date at the top changes. The release notes of the app name every such change.
